## The Medical Strategy: Using Desmopressin (DDAVP)

For isolated overnight events like sleepovers or summer camp, medication is often highly effective. **Desmopressin (DDAVP)** is a synthetic version of vasopressin, the hormone that naturally slows down urine production at night. 

When taken before bed, it reduces the volume of urine the kidneys produce, helping the bladder hold whatever is generated until morning. 

> **Clinical Pearl:** If using Desmopressin, you *must* strictly limit fluid intake in the evening. Taking DDAVP and drinking excess water can lead to hyponatremia (dangerously low sodium levels). Always follow your prescriber's exact instructions regarding fluid safety.

## A Three-Layer Practical Plan

Medication provides internal security, but practical preparations ensure dignity and peace of mind. 

### Layer One: Supplies and Routines
Pack discreet, absorbent underwear (such as Pull-Ups). A highly effective trick for camps and sleepovers is placing the absorbent underwear inside the bottom of the sleeping bag beforehand. The teen can slip into the sleeping bag and pull them on privately, then reverse the process in the morning, leaving the used product hidden in the bag until it can be discreetly disposed of in a small waterproof pouch.

Incorporate **dietary restrictions** strictly while at camp or a sleepover: avoid bladder irritants like caffeine (colas, chocolate), sugary drinks, and citrus in the evening. Additionally, practice **double voiding** by peeing once after dinner and a second time right before slipping into the sleeping bag.

### Layer Two: Trusted Adults and Psychological Triggers
Identify one trusted adult - a camp nurse, counselor, or the host parent. You do not need to explain every detail. A simple script works best: "I have a nighttime medical issue and may need private access to my bag or the bathroom. Please keep this confidential." 

Keep in mind that the stress of being away from home can act as a **psychological trigger**, potentially worsening bedwetting even in teens who have been mostly dry. Establishing this safety net with an adult greatly reduces anxiety.

### Layer Three: Contingencies
Have a simple, rehearsed sequence if an accident occurs: wake, change privately, place wet items in a waterproof bag, and contact the trusted adult if assistance is needed. 

Bedwetting is a medical symptom, and 15% of children outgrow it each year. Active treatment expedites this, but until then, a teen does not have to sacrifice their social life. Preparation protects dignity.
